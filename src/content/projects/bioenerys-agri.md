## Investment brief

Vesper asked for a European investment opportunity with a €100m equity cheque, a control position, strong capital protection features and a value-creation initiative targeting a 20% gross IRR over five years.

I proposed the acquisition of Bioenerys Agri, Snam's Italian agricultural biomethane platform. The case centres on completing 16 biogas-to-biomethane conversions, then selling an operating portfolio with remaining contracted tariff income. It was prepared in October 2026 using public information and explicit modelling assumptions.

The proposal fits Vesper's clean-energy and circular-economy themes. Its existing investments in [BioHold](https://vesperam.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Vesper-BioHold-PR-Final.pdf) and [EAG Bioenergy](https://vesperam.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Vesper-ONC-EAGB-announcement-24-October-2024.pdf) provide relevant precedents for the operating strategy.

## Acquisition and financing

The proposed €130m enterprise value comprises an assumed €40m share purchase and repayment of €90m of Snam intercompany loans. The closing funding package combines €100m of equity with a €95m senior term loan, covering the acquisition, fees, reserves and €45.7m retained for development.

A separate capex facility bridges construction spending and grant receipts. The base case models a peak draw of €23.6m and repayment by 2029. The €95m term loan represents 4.7 times estimated closing EBITDA, with debt falling as the portfolio starts distributing cash.

The financial starting point distinguishes reported revenue for an individual legal entity from reconstructed plant-level earnings. The estimated closing run rate is €72.1m of revenue and €20.2m of EBITDA.

## Operational value creation

The assumed acquisition perimeter contains 30 plants: eight producing biomethane, 16 undergoing conversion and six remaining as biogas engines. The plan commissions eight conversions in July 2027 and eight in March 2028, bringing the biomethane fleet to 24 plants.

The model budgets €109.4m of remaining development expenditure and assumes €51.7m of PNRR grants. Converted capacity, feedstock procurement and reliability improvements lift modelled EBITDA to €50.3m in 2031. Cost inflation offsets part of the operating gains because the tariffs are fixed in nominal terms.

The operating plan links expenditure to commissioning milestones and plant output. Methane-leak controls, sustainability certification and feedstock compliance protect tariff eligibility as well as operating performance.

## Returns and downside

The base case produces a modelled **19.8% gross IRR and 2.25× money multiple** across a five-year hold. Interim distributions contribute €84.1m, followed by approximately €141m of sale proceeds in 2031.

| Modelled outcome | Base | Bull | Bear |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gross IRR | 19.8% | 21.7% | 4.7% |
| Money multiple | 2.25× | 2.39× | 1.25× |

The €202.4m base-case exit enterprise value uses a 7% DCF discount rate on remaining tariff-period cash flows, with no value counted after tariff expiry. Its implied 4.0× EBITDA multiple is below the 6.4× entry multiple.

The bear case tests delayed commissioning, two plants missing the incentive deadline, a 12% capex overrun, higher operating costs and an 8% exit discount rate. These are modelled scenarios, not guarantees of capital protection.

## Assumptions and sources

The acquisition price, plant split, financing terms, conversion costs and grant eligibility are assumptions. The investment depends particularly on confirming each concession, eligible grant costs and contractor delivery commitments. The deck estimates that removing the grants reduces gross IRR to approximately 13%.
