# How I approach research. Each point links to a piece of work that shows it.
approach:
  - title: Start with the physical system
    text: >-
      Prices respond to barrels and cargoes that can actually move. I begin with flows, capacity
      and inventories, then ask what they mean for price.
    example:
      label: Saudi crude rerouting to Yanbu
      href: /research/saudi-crude-rerouting-yanbu/
  - title: Keep data, estimates and assumptions apart
    text: >-
      Agencies use different definitions and reporting periods. I identify each source and check
      for overlapping constraints before combining figures.
    example:
      label: The distillate bottleneck
      href: /research/diesel-distillate-bottleneck/
  - title: Use scenarios, with ranges
    text: >-
      A range tied to a stated physical condition is more useful than a point forecast that
      implies precision the evidence does not support.
    example:
      label: Oil Above 100
      href: /research/oil-above-100-supply-shock/
  - title: Say what would change the view
    text: >-
      My longer reports set out, in advance, the evidence that would weaken the thesis and the
      evidence that would strengthen it.
    example:
      label: Panama Canal and Asian energy costs
      href: /research/panama-canal-el-nino-asia-energy-costs/

I co-founded 6:05 Markets and have been its co-president since April 2026. It is a student-led research group at the University of Chicago that publishes weekly work on commodities, macroeconomics and policy. I help lead its research and editorial side: I set the standards analysts write to, built the report templates and publication process, and write on oil and gas myself.

The name refers to the moment five minutes after a headline, when the first reaction has begun to settle and the more useful questions begin. That is the kind of research I want to write.

Before 6:05 Markets I led the energy team at UChicago Commodities. In the summer of 2026 I interned in wealth services at HBL Bank UK in London, where I wrote cross-asset market outlooks and worked on fee benchmarking and tax analysis. Since October 2026 I have been a research contributor at Energy Flux, where I am developing independently authored research on the US natural gas market for publication.

The questions I find most interesting sit where physical constraints meet prices: a strait, a canal, a refinery outage, a pipeline running at capacity. I try to work out what the market has already priced in, and what it would take to prove me wrong.
