keyFindings:
  - >-
    An auction premium buys access; it does not replace the toll. Under the published tariff a
    3.9 million MMBtu LNG carrier would pay a base toll of roughly US$648,500. The US$5.3
    million bid in September was an exceptional scarcity premium for immediate passage.
  - >-
    Paying, waiting and rerouting are competing options. The EIA estimates that a US Gulf to
    East Asia propane shipment takes about a month through Panama and around two weeks longer
    via the Cape of Good Hope. The choice depends on the value of time, and the three costs
    should not be added together.
  - >-
    Freight had already repriced. BLNG3, the US Gulf to Japan LNG freight route, rose from
    US$29,400 a day on 4 September to US$66,000 on 25 September. By 2 October it had eased to
    US$59,100, still about double its early-September level.
  - >-
    The marginal cargo sets the Asian price. A rise of US$0.25–1.00 per MMBtu in logistics
    costs does not move JKM by the same amount. When inventories are being drawn and
    replacement cargoes are scarce, a buyer may have to pay more than the transport cost to
    divert the next cargo.
  - >-
    Oil is a secondary channel. Fully laden VLCCs do not transit the canal, so the report
    expected crude to be affected through freight, product prices and competition for
    replacement barrels: roughly US$3–6 a barrel of incremental support in the severe case,
    assuming an otherwise unchanged global balance.

Renewed Panama restrictions would compound an already tight energy system. The Panama Canal Authority cut daily transit slots from 36 to 32 in September 2026, then announced 33 from 15 October and raised the permitted draught to 14.94 metres. Our base case assumed that this relief would prove temporary and that capacity would tighten again to roughly 28–30 transits a day through the fourth quarter as a strong El Niño developed.

We expected the clearest effect in freight and in delivered LNG and LPG prices in Asia, with outright Brent a secondary channel. The base case placed JKM at US$27–31 per MMBtu, BLNG3 at US$70,000–90,000 a day and BLPG3 at US$350–400 a tonne through late Q4 2026. Confidence was medium-high on freight and medium on LNG and LPG prices.

The thesis depended on the recent improvement in rainfall and operating conditions proving temporary. The report identifies the freight, auction and transit levels that would weaken it.
