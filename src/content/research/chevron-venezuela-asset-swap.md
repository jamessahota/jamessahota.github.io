Chevron agreed to exchange upstream and downstream assets in Venezuela with PDVSA. It raised its stake in the Petroindependencia heavy-oil joint venture to 49% and added Ayacucho 8, and gave up the Loran gas field and a small oil interest in western Venezuela. The note reads this as a restructuring of existing holdings. Chevron concentrates on heavy crude that suits its refineries and its existing operations, and gives up gas that would be slower and more complex to commercialise.

Our position was moderately positive. The swap could lift Chevron’s Venezuelan production by about 50%, from 250,000 to 390,000 barrels a day within two years. We argued that the market should not treat it as a major valuation re-rating, because legal uncertainty, sanctions exposure and weak infrastructure place a ceiling on the story.
