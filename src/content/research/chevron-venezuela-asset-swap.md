Chevron agreed to exchange upstream and downstream assets in Venezuela with PDVSA. It raised its stake in the Petroindependencia heavy-oil joint venture to 49% and added Ayacucho 8, while giving up the Loran gas field and a small oil interest in western Venezuela. The swap concentrates Chevron’s existing holdings in heavy crude suited to its refineries, in exchange for gas that would be slower and more complex to commercialise.

Our position was moderately positive. The swap could lift Chevron’s Venezuelan production by about 50%, from 250,000 to 390,000 barrels a day within two years. Legal uncertainty, sanctions exposure and weak infrastructure limited the case for a major valuation re-rating.
