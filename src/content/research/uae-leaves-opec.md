The UAE left OPEC and OPEC+ with effect from 1 May 2026, after a dispute over a quota of 3.4 million barrels a day that sat well below the capacity it had invested in. The brief argues that the exit was not an immediate bearish supply release. The market was still dominated by Gulf shipping risk, and the UAE’s bypass pipeline was expected to be running at or near capacity.

Our overall view was that Brent would stay supported near US$115–130 a barrel while Hormuz risk persisted, then move towards US$90–105 if UAE barrels re-entered a normalised shipping market without offsetting Saudi cuts. The longer-term implication was a weaker and less cohesive OPEC framework.
