The brief looks at how the Iran war and the disruption at Hormuz changed the case for wind power in Europe. France launched a 12 GW renewable tender round, including 10 GW of offshore wind, with criteria that favour European-sourced components. Germany raised its onshore wind auction volumes and described energy policy as security policy. In the UK, wind supplied around 42% of power in the first three months of 2026, against around 33% a year earlier, which reduced the system’s exposure to gas prices.

Our position was that wind is increasingly valued as a source of energy that is less exposed to geopolitical disruption, in addition to its role in decarbonisation. The outlook was positive for the following few years, with permitting and grid bottlenecks as the main obstacles.
